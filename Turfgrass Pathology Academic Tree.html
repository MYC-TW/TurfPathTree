For two programs here — the CDGA and Ohio State — only the succession of posts is documented, so those lines carry no advising edges at all. The dotted violet line is informal mentorship: trained under someone without that person signing the degree. Bands group people by career stage rather than by strict generation, so plenty of edges run sideways within a band — Kerns advised Roberts, Kerns and Koch each hold posts the other once held — and those curve below the row rather than down to the next one.

Sources

    Exactly one link here rests on surmise rather than evidence: the pair of Arkansas co-advisors on Nathan Walker's doctorate, carried at the unverified level and drawn with a dashed node outline to say so. Seven more are dashed as reported — strongly evidenced, but without a source that states the relationship outright. The gaps that are left point upward rather than across: McCall's doctoral advisor at Virginia Tech, Vargas's at Minnesota, Mitkowski's at Cornell, Tomaso-Peterson's at Mississippi State, Walker's master's advisor at Clemson, Latin's own training. Each would extend a line back a generation rather than add anyone new.

    Two patterns show up once the graph is this complete. Turf pathology is entered more often than inherited: Uddin, Kennelly and Shaohui Wu all trained in other systems and specialized afterwards, so their doctoral edges run clean out of the field. And the destination is increasingly not a university — thirteen people here are in industry, five of them at Envu alone, the Maryland chair has lost two holders in a row to it, and of Clarke's students in this diagram more are now at companies than at universities.
